# Interview prep sprints

LoopLoom offers two short tracks built from its existing guided lessons. The one-week track has 25 problems: **20 Easy and 5 Medium**. The last-day track has 7 recall problems selected from the same set, so attempts and progress carry across both tracks and the larger roadmaps.

## Selection method

- [Grind 75](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/grind75/) supplies the priority backbone. Its author [explains](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/grind75/about) that popularity, frequency, topic coverage, and learner value informed the ranking. We keep its early high-priority easy problems that already have LoopLoom lessons.
- Five medium problems add array construction, sliding windows, maximum subarray reasoning, graph traversal, and intervals. They are Grind 75 problems and cover transfer beyond easy exercises.
- [LeetCode's Top Interview 150](https://leetcode.com/studyplan/top-interview-150/) supports broad topic coverage but explicitly describes its full track as best for three or more months. These sprints are intentionally narrower.

Interview question frequency varies by company, role, and date. These tracks are a prioritized practice set, not a prediction or a claim of proprietary company frequency data. LoopLoom's coaching text remains independently authored; official problem statements are linked from each lesson.

The requested 80/20 Easy-to-Medium mix is a fast fundamentals track. [Grind 75's FAQ](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/grind75/faq) says Medium is the most common coding-interview difficulty and places Easy questions early to teach reusable patterns. The app therefore offers a direct continuation into the Medium-filtered Blind 75 after this sprint. Intermediate candidates should use it when they can already solve the Easy problems reliably.

## One-week schedule

1. Hashing: Two Sum, Valid Anagram, Contains Duplicate, Ransom Note.
2. Strings and arrays: Best Time to Buy and Sell Stock, Valid Palindrome, Valid Parentheses, Product of Array Except Self.
3. Linked lists and windows: Merge Two Sorted Lists, Reverse Linked List, Linked List Cycle, Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters.
4. Search and trees: Binary Search, First Bad Version, Invert Binary Tree, Maximum Depth of Binary Tree.
5. Recursive and running state: Balanced Binary Tree, Lowest Common Ancestor of a Binary Search Tree, Diameter of Binary Tree, Maximum Subarray.
6. Traversal and state: Majority Element, Climbing Stairs, Single Number, Number of Islands.
7. Review weak spots, then Merge Intervals.

## Last-day recall

Two Sum, Valid Parentheses, Binary Search, Reverse Linked List, Invert Binary Tree, Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters, and Number of Islands. Explain the approach aloud and trace an edge case before writing code.
